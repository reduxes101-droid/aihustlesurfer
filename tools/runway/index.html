Runway review: impressive clips, expensive minutes

Runway produces five-to-ten-second clips that can pass as stock footage, which is a real asset for a faceless channel. Users consistently report needing several attempts per usable clip, and every attempt costs credits. Whether that is worth it depends on how much B-roll you need.

AIHustleSurfer Editorial · Updated Aug 6, 2026 · 2 min read

What works

  • Image-to-video output is stable enough to use as real B-roll
  • Camera controls and motion brush give actual direction over the shot
  • Clips are commercially usable on paid plans
  • Fast turnaround, typically a couple of minutes per clip

What doesn't

  • A meaningful share of generations are unusable and still cost credits
  • Credits do not roll over, so quiet months are wasted money
  • Hands, text and fast motion remain weak points
  • The top Max plan is $95 a month, a lot for B-roll

What it is

Runway is a generative video platform. Give it a text prompt or a still image and it produces a short clip. It also offers editing tools, background removal and lip sync, but video generation is what people pay for.

Everything runs on credits. Each second of generated video costs a set number, plans include a monthly allowance, and more can be bought. Monthly plan credits expire; purchased top-up credits do not. The former Unlimited plan has been replaced by a credit-capped Max plan.

What it is used for

B-roll for narrated channels: money changing hands, skylines, people at laptops, abstract motion behind talking points. The most recommended approach is to animate a still from Midjourney rather than prompt video from scratch, because image-to-video is markedly more stable.

Agencies use it for social clips and concept previews. Filmmakers use it for previsualisation.

Where it stands out

Control. Camera movement settings and the motion brush, which lets you paint which parts of a still should move, mean you can direct a shot instead of hoping. That is the difference between a tool and a slot machine, and it is where Runway is usually rated above cheaper rivals.

Image-to-video quality. A well-lit still with a gentle push-in or pan comes out looking like licensed stock. For a channel needing many clips per video, that can replace a stock subscription.

Where it falls short

The hit rate. Warped limbs, motion that drifts into nonsense in the final seconds, and unusable outputs are common enough that users routinely describe a real cost per usable clip of two to three times the sticker price. On Standard, careless use can exhaust the allowance in a few weeks.

Credit expiry. Miss a month of publishing and the plan credits are gone; only purchased top-ups carry over. The Max plan's 9,500 credits at $95 only make sense at a daily cadence, and there is no longer an unlimited relaxed mode to fall back on.

Detail. Hands, on-screen text and fast action still break. Keep prompts to slow, simple motion.

Who it suits

Channel operators publishing at least weekly who want B-roll that does not look like everyone else's stock library. Start on Standard, animate stills, and track your usable-clip rate before upgrading.

Occasional publishers are better served by a stock footage subscription. For a presenter rather than B-roll, see HeyGen.
