ChatGPT review: the safest all-rounder, not the best writer

ChatGPT Plus is the closest thing to one subscription that handles writing, images, voice and research. That breadth is the reason to buy it. It is also why the writing tends to need a heavier edit, according to most published comparisons.

What it is

ChatGPT is OpenAI's assistant and the product most people mean when they say "AI". It writes, codes, generates images, talks in voice mode, searches the web and reads uploaded files. The free tier includes most of that with caps and, since February 2026, ads. Go is a cheap ad-supported step up. Plus lifts the caps, removes ads and unlocks the stronger models. Pro is one plan sold in two usage tiers, $100 and $200 a month, built for heavy reasoning and technical workloads.

This overview looks at it for one purpose: earning with it. That means writing, images for pins and thumbnails, research, and the admin tasks that fill a freelancer's day.

What it is used for

Everything, which is the point. Client blog drafts, email sequences, thumbnail concepts, video descriptions, turning rough notes into something sendable. Voice mode for dictating outlines gets more use than people expect.

Image generation is the surprise workhorse for creators. For Pinterest backgrounds and thumbnails that need legible text, it is more practical than Midjourney because it can spell. The images are less beautiful, which matters less at thumbnail size.

Where it stands out

Breadth. No other single subscription covers this much. For someone starting out who can pay for one thing, it is the defensible choice.

Research with current examples. Asking for recent case studies returns them with links. Claude can search now too, but ChatGPT is generally reported as faster and more willing to pull in specifics.

Memory. It retains your niche, tone and clients across chats, so the preamble disappears after a few weeks.

Where it falls short

Writing. It is good, and closing the gap, but published side-by-sides on real briefs keep finding the same things: drafts longer than requested, a rhetorical question to open, a summary to close after being told not to. Each is fixable with a prompt. Each costs time on every job.

Cadence. Three-item lists, "it's not just X, it's Y", tidy conclusions. Clients recognise it. A banned-phrases list in custom instructions helps; users of Claude report needing it less.

Image queue times. Ten-minute waits at busy hours are widely reported. If pins are the business, that is a real cost.

Who it suits

Beginners, generalists, and anyone who wants images and text under one login. Plus at $20 is good value for that person.

Someone who already pays for Claude for writing probably does not need this as well. The free tier covers occasional images and second opinions.
